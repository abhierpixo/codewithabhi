## What only you can fill in afterwards

The prompt will leave `[TODO: number]` markers. These are the ones worth chasing
before you publish:

- **Your first 5 reviews.** Offer a handful of free 30-minute sessions to people
  in your network in exchange for an honest public review. An empty review
  section is the single biggest conversion killer on these platforms.
- **Real learner outcomes.** Anyone you've already trained who switched roles or
  got placed — with their permission, one line each.
- **Your rates.** The prompt suggests bands; you decide. Common starting point for
  a 14-year engineer in India is ₹500–1,500 for 30 minutes, more for interviews
  and reviews.
- **Availability.** Realistic slots around a full-time job — evenings and
  weekends only is fine and better than over-promising.

## Fix these inconsistencies first

Buyers cross-check profiles, and mismatches read as carelessness.

- **13+ vs 14+ years.** Your GitHub bio says "13+ years of experience"; this
  site and the Topmate prompt say 14+. Pick one and make it true everywhere.
- **GitHub is thin.** 2 public repos, 0 followers, account opened April 2025. For
  a 14-year engineer that reads worse than having no GitHub link at all. Either
  leave it off the profile, or give it something real first — the sanitised
  extracts we discussed (a Go matching engine, an OpenAI Vision pipeline) plus a
  proper profile README. Until then, link LinkedIn and the App Store instead.
- **Erpixo LLP** is listed as your *company* on your GitHub profile, but you
  contributed to it rather than owning it. Fix that field — an employer name you
  don't hold is the kind of thing a buyer or recruiter checks and misreads.

## One thing to check before publishing

Read your employment contract's moonlighting and outside-activity clause. A paid
services marketplace with your name and photo is more visible than a personal
site, and some Indian tech employers require written disclosure of paid outside
work even when it doesn't compete. Worth ten minutes now.
